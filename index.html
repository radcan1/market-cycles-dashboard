Market Cycles — Tuesday 21 July 2026

This page tracks 30 major investments — stocks, bonds, gold, oil and more — and flags when any looks stretched, either near a market top (overheated, at risk of falling) or near a market bottom (beaten-down, possibly a bargain). Each investment gets two scores from 0 to 100 — one for top risk, one for bottom opportunity — and the higher the score, the stronger the warning. Every score comes in two versions: a broad read that counts every signal equally, and a validated read that counts only the signals with a proven track record on that exact investment. The validated read says "none" wherever no signal has earned that trust yet, which is true for most investments. When the two disagree, trust the validated one.

As of Tuesday 21 July 2026, the most reliable signals — the few that have a real track record of spotting past market tops — are warning that some investments look overheated, close to a possible top: US small cap (99 out of 100), Global equities (99 out of 100) and Industrials (96 out of 100). The same kind of reliable signal flags Technology (52 out of 100) as looking beaten-down, close to a possible bottom. These stand out even though most other measures look calm right now. Each of these warnings is based on only a handful of past examples, so treat them as the best evidence available, not a sure thing. Everything below this summary is the supporting detail.

Method and caveats

This page estimates how close each asset class is to conditions that have historically accompanied major tops and bottoms. It does not predict prices, and it says nothing about any portfolio: it holds no information about holdings, allocations or investment policy, by design.

Tops and bottoms behave differently — bottoms tend to be sharp, fear-driven events while tops are long rolling processes — so the two gauges are scored separately and never combined into a single verdict.

Two readings sit side by side. The breadth reading (top risk and bottom opportunity) weights every indicator equally, so it summarises what the indicators collectively say rather than proving that any one works. The validated reading counts only indicators that passed the event-study engine on that specific asset — measured against an era-matched base rate, checked out of sample, and discounted for resting on few firings — weighted by the edge they earned. It reads "none" wherever nothing has yet earned its place, which is most assets, and that emptiness is deliberately shown rather than hidden. Where the two disagree, the validated reading is the one to trust; the breadth reading is context. Each indicator's full track record, including the rejections, is in the per-asset detail and the validation scorecard.

Full method: see the design specification and the implementation specification in the docs folder of this project.
